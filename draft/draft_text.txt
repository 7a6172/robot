        V. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION OF THE TRANSFER-FIRST PARADIGM
    We instantiate the NMTE program on a differential-drive
navigation benchmark (Isaac Lab/Isaac Sim, ROS 2) and evaluate
its central proposition — a policy trained ONCE in a source
world is redeployed to unseen worlds by adapting interfaces
only, weights frozen — along the geometry, dynamics, safety,
and task/deployment axes. The source policy is trained solely
in 8 m rooms with 4-12 scattered walls; every result below uses
the same frozen checkpoint, and every negative is reported
alongside the positives.

A. Zero-Shot Transfer Across Geometry: a Gradient with a
   Measured Boundary
    On a designed difficulty ladder (same spawn/goal, same
13.2 m path), the frozen policy solves the easy rung (scattered
walls) in 29.3 s and the mid rung (full-width staggered
partitions, a map class absent from training) in 26.2 s —
faster than easy, because the transferred primitives
(gap-threading, contact recovery) are even funneled by the
partitions. The hard rung (gaps offset off the direct line)
fails with a corner-wedge signature; narrow-corridor and far-
gap maps fail likewise. Transfer is thus not monotone in visual
complexity but in behavioural overlap with the source
distribution, and its residual boundary localizes to a specific
behaviour (approach angle at narrow passages), not to "maze
complexity". Four seeded repeats with spawn jitter (+-0.15 m,
+-15 deg) succeed 4/4 (20.9-33.5 s): the positive side of the
gradient is robust, not a lucky seed.

B. Interface Ablations: What Does the Adapting
    Blinding the geodesic-waypoint channel (zeroing the
interface observation) on the same map turns 4/4 success into
0/1 (final gap 5.7 m): the math-guided interface is load-
bearing for transfer — the frozen LiDAR reflexes alone do not
compose into gap-threading. On the dynamics axis, a
disturbance-observer interface recovers tracking under degraded
friction with frozen weights (DOB-lite A/B); on the safety
axis, a QP CBF filter enforces bounds without touching the
policy (CBF-lite A/B); and an offline Koopman operator fit in
the source world, kept frozen, closes degraded-regime
prediction error by refitting only the lightweight lifting
statistics — the dynamics-side analogue of "weights frozen,
interface adapts".

C. Task Structure and Deployment Axes
    Without retraining, the same frozen policy composes into an
ordered three-goal tour (46.1 s, 3 brief recoveries) via goal
sequencing and per-leg field rebuilds at the interface level —
transfer across task structure, not just geometry. The policy
deploys through plain ROS 2 topics with a single map file as
the shared world model of simulator and controller, and the
same interface drives a second embodiment (pioneer3dx),
evidencing cross-embodiment deployability of the interface
layer pending full GW latent alignment.

D. Framework-Arm Tally and Honest Gaps
    Koopman core: frozen source fit, few-shot lift refit
(offline validated). DOB-lite / CBF-lite: A/B-validated at
deploy time. Geodesic waypoint interface: load-bearing
(ablation). Recovery layer: quantified behavioural fallback
(3-25 events per run), whose wedge failure motivates bearing-
aware CBF shaping. Serial composition: demonstrated. Honest
gaps remain: the GW cross-embodiment arm is not yet validated
beyond a deployment smoke test, and most geometry results are
single-seed demos by design; the ladder and seed repeats form
the qualitative gradient, while the transfer table and
adaptation-budget curve remain the quantitative backbone.

                       VI. CONCLUSION
    We argued that the reality gap is best attacked transfer-
first: teach the simple behaviour once, and let mathematical
interfaces carry it into new worlds. On a frozen differential-
drive policy we showed that learning can be transferred —
across unseen geometry (a measured gradient, 4/4 under spawn
jitter), across task structure (three-goal tour), across
dynamics and safety axes (DOB-lite/CBF-lite A/B), and toward a
second embodiment — while ablations localize what makes the
transfer work (the geodesic interface) and what remains to be
covered (bearing-aware recovery at narrow passages). The NMTE
components (Koopman core, DOB, CBF) are offered as validated-
in-part concepts whose idea — frozen learned core, lightweight
certifiable interface — is the contribution. Future work: few-
shot lift refits in closed loop, CBF-shaped recoveries, and
hardware validation on the edge.
